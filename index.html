The Problem

Human judgment is a critical layer of cybersecurity.

Even with email filtering, endpoint protection, firewalls, and detection systems, the user remains a critical decision point. Phishing exploits everyday electronic communication — every link, attachment, and request demands an immediate judgment.

Traditional cybersecurity awareness training relies on presentations, informational materials, and static quizzes. Such approaches transmit basic knowledge, but do not necessarily ensure that a user can apply that knowledge when confronted with a genuinely suspicious message.

The core pedagogical principle

“Awareness should be transformed into a repeatedly practised skill.”

Rather than merely informing users of the risks, PhishGame places them in the role of an analyst: examine realistic emails, identify critical elements, evaluate them, justify their decision, and assume responsibility for the final judgment.

The Core Experience — Inbox Mission

The five sequential stages of each round

01

Examine the email

The system selects a message corresponding to the user's level. The sender, subject, and body are to be examined carefully.

02

Annotate indicators

Any part of the sender, subject, or body may be selected. A popover presents Safe or Malicious.

03

Justify assessment

A free-text justification is evaluated by an LLM or deterministic algorithm assessing concept coverage.

04

Declare confidence

Low (×0.5), Medium (×1.0), or High (×1.5). The multiplier affects both gains and losses.

05

Issue judgment

Choose Phishing or Legitimate. Receive immediate breakdown of correct and missed indicators.

Scoring Engine

Five independent signals

Performance is not confined to whether the final answer was correct or incorrect; it is derived from a multidimensional engine that rewards the entire decision-making process.

Decision
Correct phishing: +100. Correct legitimate: +100. False positive: −60. False negative: −120.
Indicators
Correctly identified and classified indicators are rewarded according to their weight; misclassifications and omissions incur penalties.
Time
Correct decision under 10 s: +30. Under 25 s: +15. Incorrect, very fast decision: −20.
Reasoning
S_R = 30R − 20(1−R), where R ∈ [0,1], yielding a range from −20 to +30.
Bonus
+25 when all malicious indicators have been identified and correctly classified, with no incorrect annotations and a correct final judgment.

Evidence Gate

A correct final judgment is not sufficient for a full score. The user must provide at least one correctly identified piece of evidence, or achieve a reasoning score of at least 0.5.

Without sufficient evidence, the reward for a correct decision is limited from 100 to 40 points; penalties for incorrect decisions are not capped.

Combo Multiplier

Consecutive correct and evidence-supported answers build a combo multiplier:

1 ×1.02 ×1.253 ×1.54 ×1.755+ ×2.0

An incorrect answer resets the combo. Anti-abuse: selections over 160 characters or 24 words are ignored, and the total reward has a lower bound of −250.

Educational Indicators

Six categories of phishing indicators

fake_domain

Fake and lookalike domains, character substitutions, and deceptive addresses.

credential_request

Requests for passwords, PINs, card numbers, and other confidential credentials.

suspicious_link

Inspection of the real URL, hover behaviour, URL mismatch, and naked IP addresses.

urgency

Artificial urgency and pressure to take immediate action.

attachment_risk

Suspicious .zip, .exe, .html files, macros, and other attachments.

minor_hint

Greetings, spelling, signatures, and other secondary indications.

User performance across these categories generates EduTips — three targeted educational cards. The weakest category is presented first as “Your Focus”, and two further low-performing categories complete the recommended training.

Competitive Real-Time Training

The Deathmatch Arena

The competitive mode of PhishGame transforms a training session into a shared phishing-analysis activity. Only one Arena may be active at any given time; activating a new round automatically terminates the previous one and publishes its results.

Deathmatch

The instructor selects a difficulty level (or any level) and a language. The server selects a specific email from the corresponding pool, and all participants view exactly the same message simultaneously — establishing the conditions for objective comparison.

Blitz

Participants work independently within a predefined time window. After each submission, the next email is loaded automatically following a short 2.5-second delay, and each email may be attempted only once. The emphasis is on speed, pattern recognition, and consistency.

Custom

Mechanically similar to Deathmatch, but the instructor selects the specific email to be used — particularly useful for connecting the Arena to a specific lesson or incident presented earlier in the training.

Arena Duration

A timer for every rhythm

30s 1 min 2 min 5 min 15 s – 60 min custom

The default durations are 30 seconds, 1 minute, 2 minutes, and 5 minutes, while custom durations from 15 seconds to 60 minutes are also supported.

Participation

Mandatory or optional

Mandatory

The Arena overlay appears automatically for participants when the round begins — suitable for organised sessions requiring universal participation.

Optional

A banner presents the options “Join Deathmatch” or “Not Now”. Declining applies only to the specific round, providing greater flexibility for optional activities.

The same engine, without exception. The Arena employs exactly the same scoring engine as standard play; there are no special Arena multipliers. The Evidence Gate, confidence multiplier, combo multiplier, indicator scoring, time bonus, reasoning score, and anti-abuse mechanisms all operate under identical rules.
Winning Conditions

Three ways to determine the winner

Fastest Catch

The winner is the first player to submit a correct judgment. In the event of a tie, the highest score prevails.

Highest Score

The winner is the player with the highest overall score. In the event of a tie, the fastest time prevails.

Fastest After Score

Priority is given to the first correct player; where no correct identification occurs, ranking is based on score.

When no participant satisfies the winning condition, the platform does not invent a winner. Instead, it displays a message indicating that there was no correct identification in that round.

Educator Control Room

Seven sections — everything an instructor requires

01

Overview

Number of players, corpus size, attempts, success rate, radial gauges, donut charts, level distributions, and live activity.

02

Players

Per-user detection accuracy, average decision time, false-positive and false-negative rates, and performance at high levels.

03

Logs

Up to 400 records, filterable by user, outcome, and email type. Expand for full scoring breakdown and CSV export.

04

Analytics

Group Awareness Profile, performance by level, decision-time histograms, rolling accuracy, and indicator capture rates.

05

Comms

Broadcast to the group or to individuals (info, warning, or critical) and drive live challenges and countdown timers.

06

Corpus

Create, generate, import, export, and manage training emails and their indicators across difficulty tiers.

07

Config

Select AI/LLM or deterministic grading, and define provider, model, base URL, and API key with live connection test.

Analytics

Rolling accuracy allows the group's performance to be monitored across the last fifty attempts using a window of ten, while indicator capture rates reveal which categories are the most difficult to recognise.

Systematic recording of results enables the identification of patterns — supporting targeted interventions and the measurement of improvement.

Platform Security

The platform enforces role separation between Player and Educator. Authentication is based on JWT, and passwords are stored using bcrypt. The educator account is predefined and not open to unrestricted registration, reducing privilege escalation risk.

The API keys of LLM providers remain on the server and are not exposed to the frontend. Deployments adapt to institutional user management and data retention requirements.

Applications

Designed for the environments most frequently targeted

Press & Media

Journalism & News Media

Scenarios reflect the daily working environment of a journalist: emails purportedly from sources, colleagues, news agencies, organisations, companies, or event organisers. Interview invitations, document deliveries, press releases, cloud-sharing notifications, and suspicious attachments may all be simulated.

The objective is not to memorise a list of rules, but to develop the ability to critically evaluate digital communication that initially appears trustworthy.

Corporate & Finance

Office Professionals

In a corporate environment, the corpus can be adapted to scenarios involving human resources, accounting, suppliers, management, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, invoices, video conferences, bank-detail changes, and requests from supervisors.

This approach is particularly important for Business Email Compromise and social-engineering attacks, in which a message may lack the classic spelling or technical errors of simple phishing examples.

Schools & Youth

Education (Ages 12–18)

The platform supports students, cybersecurity clubs, and formal or non-formal education programmes. In a typical session, the instructor uses Inbox Mission for individual practice and subsequently activates a Deathmatch Arena for a group activity.

In this manner, individual learning, feedback, competition, and performance analysis are combined.

From Awareness to Digital Resilience

Not a one-time campaign, but a continuous capability.

PhishGame does not treat awareness as an isolated campaign. Systematic recording of results allows organisations to identify patterns and to design targeted interventions, functioning as an environment for the continuous assessment and development of human cybersecurity capabilities.

The user is asked to examine a message, identify the evidence, assess its reliability, justify the decision, and assume responsibility for the final judgment.
