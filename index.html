The Problem

Human decision-making is the critical layer of cybersecurity.

Even with email filtering, endpoint protection, firewalls, and detection systems, the user remains a critical decision point. Phishing exploits the everyday dependence on electronic communication — every link, attachment, and request demands an immediate judgment.

Traditional training relies on presentations and static quizzes. They transmit knowledge — but do not ensure a user can apply it against a real suspicious message.

The core principle

“Awareness should be transformed into a repeatedly practiced skill.”

Rather than simply informing users about phishing risks, PhishGame places them in the role of an analyst: examine realistic emails, identify critical elements, justify the decision, and take responsibility for the final call.

The Core Experience — Inbox Mission

Five stages of every round

01

Examine the email

The system selects a message matched to your level and language. Read the sender, subject, and body carefully.

02

Highlight indicators

Select any part of the sender, subject, or body. A popover offers Safe or Malicious. Each annotation is stored as a structured note.

03

Justify your reasoning

Write a free-text justification, graded by an LLM or deterministic algorithm on concept coverage and indicator alignment.

04

Set your confidence

Low (×0.5), Medium (×1.0), or High (×1.5). The multiplier applies to the decision — affecting gains and losses.

05

Make the call

Choose Phishing or Legitimate. Get immediate feedback: correct indicators, missed ones, and incorrect annotations.

Scoring Engine

Five independent signals

Performance isn't limited to right-or-wrong. It's derived from a multidimensional engine that rewards the whole decision-making process.

Decision
Correct phishing: +100 · Correct legitimate: +100 · False positive: −60 · False negative: −120.
Indicators
Correctly identified and classified indicators rewarded by weight. Misclassifications and omissions incur penalties.
Time
Correct under 10s: +30 · Under 25s: +15 · Incorrect very fast decision: −20.
Reasoning
S_R = 30R − 20(1−R), where R ∈ [0,1]. Range from −20 to +30.
Bonus
+25 when all malicious indicators identified and classified, no incorrect annotations, and a correct final judgment.

Evidence Gate

A correct final judgment alone isn't enough for a full score. You must provide at least one correctly identified piece of evidence, or achieve a reasoning score ≥ 0.5.

Without sufficient evidence, a correct decision is capped from 100 to 40 points. Penalties for incorrect decisions are not capped. This discourages simply guessing.

Combo Multiplier

Consecutive correct, evidence-supported answers build a combo:

1 ×1.02 ×1.253 ×1.54 ×1.755+ ×2.0

An incorrect answer resets the combo. Anti-abuse: selections over 160 chars / 24 words are ignored, and total reward is bounded at −250.

Educational Indicators

Six phishing indicator categories

fake_domain

Fake and lookalike domains, character substitutions, and deceptive addresses.

credential_request

Requests for passwords, PINs, card numbers, and other secrets.

suspicious_link

Real URL inspection, hover behavior, URL mismatch, and naked IP addresses.

urgency

Artificial urgency and pressure to take immediate action.

attachment_risk

Suspicious .zip, .exe, .html, macros, and other attachments.

minor_hint

Greetings, spelling, signatures, and other secondary indicators.

Your performance across these categories generates EduTips — three short bilingual cards. The weakest category appears first as “Your Focus,” with two more low-performing categories completing your recommended training.

Competitive Real-Time Training

Enter the Deathmatch Arena

Transform a training session into a shared, competitive phishing-analysis activity. Only one Arena is active at a time — activating a new round auto-terminates the previous one and publishes its results.

Deathmatch

The instructor selects a difficulty (or any level) and language. The server picks one email — everyone sees exactly the same message simultaneously. Objective comparison: same problem, different strategies.

Blitz

Participants work independently within a time window. After each submission, the next email auto-loads after 2.5s. Each email is attempted once. Emphasis on speed and consistency.

Custom

Mechanically like Deathmatch, but the instructor picks the exact email — ideal for connecting the Arena to a specific lesson or incident presented earlier.

Participation

Mandatory or optional

Mandatory

The Arena overlay appears automatically when the round starts — ideal for organized sessions.

Optional

A banner offers “Join Deathmatch” or “Not Now.” Declining applies only to that round.

Same engine, no shortcuts. The Arena uses exactly the same scoring engine as solo play. No special multipliers. Evidence Gate, confidence multiplier, combo multiplier, indicator scoring, time bonus, reasoning score, and anti-abuse all apply identically — so skills built in practice transfer directly to competition.
Winning Conditions

Three ways to claim victory

Fastest Catch

The first player to submit a correct judgment wins. Ties broken by highest score.

Highest Score

The player with the highest overall score wins. Ties broken by fastest time.

Fastest After Score

Priority to the first correct player; with no correct catch, ranking falls back to score.

When no participant satisfies the winning condition, the platform doesn't invent a winner — it displays a message that there was no correct identification in that round.

Platform Security

Role separation between Player and Educator. JWT auth; passwords stored with bcrypt. The educator account is predefined — not open registration.

LLM provider API keys stay on the server, never exposed to the frontend.
